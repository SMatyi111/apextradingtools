    How to read this

    • The question it answers: "Could results like mine come from a strategy with no edge, just luck?" It looks at your average result per trade after costs, how much the results vary, and how many trades there are.
    • The verdict is the weakest reading that applies, checked in this order: average zero or negative → NO EVIDENCE; fewer than 30 trades → WEAK; p of 0.25 or more → NO EVIDENCE; not significant after the correction for strategies tried → WEAK; any failed check → LOOKS SIGNIFICANT - CHECK STABILITY & COSTS; otherwise SIGNIFICANT AND STABLE.
    • p is the chance that a strategy with zero edge would show an average at least this good. Small p means "unusual for luck", not "proven".
    • Strategies tried (N): if you tested many systems, settings or pairs and kept the best, the best one looks good by chance. The tool multiplies p by N (Bonferroni). Be honest with N; it is the setting that changes the verdict most often.
    • The checks ask whether the result depends on one half of the record, one year, five trades, low costs, or the period you designed it on. "n/a" means there is not enough data for that check; it does not count as a pass for your confidence, only for the verdict rule.
    • The luck band keeps your exact trade sizes but flips each trade's sign at random. If your real curve stays inside the grey band, luck explains it.
    • The bootstrap range resamples your own trades: it shows how much the average itself could move with a different draw of the same kind of trades.
    • How many trades you need assumes your measured average is the true edge and simulates many futures. Measured edges are usually too high (the best-looking strategy is the one you keep), so treat the number as a minimum.
    • Drawdown and risk per trade use the same simulated futures with fixed-fractional sizing. The zero-edge line matters even with a real edge: it is what sizing alone does to an account.

    What this tool does not check

    • Whether your backtest used future data (lookahead, repainting indicators, unfinished higher-timeframe bars).
    • Whether trades overlap or several pairs trade the same move; that makes results look more certain than they are.
    • Whether your live trading matches your backtest, or whether your journal left out trades.
    • Whether the broker's prices and costs in the file are the ones you will get.

    File formats

    • CSV (comma, semicolon or tab): a header row with result (or R, profit, pnl, pips), and ideally date, symbol and cost (cost per trade as a positive number). Dates like 2025-03-04 14:30 or 2025.03.04 work best.
    • APXTT journal (Premium .xlsm, Premium Demo .xlsm or Basic .xlsx): drop the journal file itself. Only closed trades are read (Check = OK); empty rows, open trades and rows the journal flags as incomplete are skipped. Premium and Demo: result = the R multiple, cost = the commission in R (the journal's R is before commission). Basic: result = Net P/L in your account currency, already after commission. Swap is not in the journal. The file must have been saved in Excel after the last change, so the results are calculated.
    • APXTT MT5 export script: a free MetaTrader 5 script that writes your closed trades, with commission, swap and fees, to a CSV this page reads. It does not trade and does not connect to the internet. (Download link coming.)
    • MT5 history report: in MetaTrader 5, History tab → right-click → Report → Open XML (.xlsx) or HTML. The Positions table is used; result = profit, cost = commission + swap. The .xlsx version was tested on a real report; the HTML version is read the same way but was not tested on a real HTML export.
    • MT5 Strategy Tester report (HTML): the Deals table is used, one trade per closing deal.
    • MT4 Detailed Statement (HTML): beta, not yet tested on a real statement. Always compare the trade count and total with your statement.

    Reports in languages other than English are not recognised yet; use the export script or a CSV.
